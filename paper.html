Abstract

Growth in e-commerce has increased package theft, delivery errors, and poor delivery updates. Smart Drop is an IoT-enabled delivery box that opens only after a one-time password is verified. Delivery partners sign in with a six-digit employee ID and use the OTP shown in the app to open a box, while customers book a box and see the OTP and order details in the same app.

The hardware couples a Raspberry Pi 3 B+ with an Arduino Uno over serial. A relay switches a 12 V electromagnetic lock, and a 3.5-inch LCD displays box state. The software is a Flutter application backed by Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore. All twelve numbered test cases and an Arduino to Raspberry Pi communication test passed. The paper also reports two open limitations: the system sends no notifications, and the lock needs continuous power to stay engaged.

Keywords: IoT, smart delivery box, OTP authentication, Flutter, Firebase Firestore, Raspberry Pi, Arduino Uno, EM lock

01Introduction

Online shopping has made doorstep delivery the norm, and the last meter of that delivery is where things break down.

Packages left unattended are frequently stolen. Manual processes produce misrouted and incorrect deliveries. Customers often have no real-time view of where their parcel is. Current systems also struggle to adapt to residential buildings, offices, and shared spaces, which each need different handling.

The Internet of Things offers a practical response. Smart Drop uses connected hardware and a cloud backend to automate access, monitor the delivery, and give both parties a shared view of the order.

1.1 The problem

The paper identifies four weaknesses in conventional delivery systems: vulnerability to theft at unattended drop points, operational inefficiency from manual handling, weak communication with the customer, and inflexibility across environments with different needs.

A secure, transparent and efficient package delivery solution is needed to meet modern demand. Problem statement, Chapter 1

1.2 Significance

For customers, a locked box with an OTP reduces theft risk and adds visibility. For businesses, fewer re-deliveries and insurance claims lower costs and protect brand reputation. More broadly, the project aims to set a benchmark for secure delivery in residential and commercial settings.

02Requirements and solution approach

The system has two halves that meet in the cloud. A Flutter mobile app serves both user roles, Firebase handles identity and real-time data, and a Raspberry Pi with an Arduino Uno controls the physical lock.

03Design and implementation

3.1 Hardware architecture

The Raspberry Pi is the central processor. It talks to the Arduino over serial, drives the LCD, and synchronises with the cloud. The Arduino switches the relay, which in turn powers the 12 V electromagnetic lock.

Wiring follows the report's connection notes. The Arduino's TX, RX, and ground pins connect to the Raspberry Pi's RX, TX, and ground. The relay's control input comes from pin 7 of the Arduino, and the relay is powered from the Arduino's 5 V pin through a breadboard. The EM lock sits on the relay's output and is fed by a 12 V supply, and the Raspberry Pi runs from a 5 V supply.

3.2 Software: the two interfaces

The Flutter application is organised around the two user groups. Each group has its own entry route from the landing screen.

Customer flow. Customers sign up with name, email, phone and password, and then log in through Firebase Authentication. From the order screen they can book a box by entering a delivery address and selecting an order. The order is written to Firestore, and the order details and OTP are shown on a summary screen.

Delivery partner flow. Partners register with their name, six-digit employee ID, phone and password. Once logged in, they see the orders waiting for pickup. Each order card offers a Get OTP action to open the box, a Completed action to mark delivery, and a Map action to view the pickup address.

3.3 Data model

Firestore holds five collections. Users store email, name and phone. Addresses store apartment, city, state, country, zone and timestamp. Orders hold the box ID, OTP, pickup address, user ID and timestamp. Delivery stores partner employee ID, name and phone. Completed orders keep the box ID, phone and pickup address once a delivery is closed. Authentication stores each user's email and UID.

3.4 Known issues

Three issues came out of implementation. The app does not yet send notifications by email, phone or any other channel. The EM lock needs a continuous supply to remain locked, so a delivery partner needs a backup supply during pickup and drop. The app also uses stateful widgets where stateless ones would be lighter, which increases memory use and battery drain.

4.1 Error handling

Failed OTP entry prompts a retry or generates a new code. Sensors or locks that fail to respond are flagged and reported. During network loss, essential data is stored locally and synced once the connection returns. Invalid box IDs or delivery details raise an exception for correction, and input fields are validated in real time.

4.2 Limitations

The system has no notification channel, so customers and partners are not alerted when a delivery moves on. The EM lock depends on continuous power, so an outage needs an external backup to keep the box secure. Both limits are real constraints on the current prototype rather than theoretical concerns.

05Findings and future work

The OTP gate worked as intended in testing and reduced unauthorised access to packages. Firebase delivered real-time updates across both interfaces. The hardware performed reliably in controlled conditions but showed limits under sustained use or harsh environments.

Future iterations are planned in four areas. Notifications would keep both parties informed of each step. Offline support would let the box accept OTPs locally during connectivity loss. Battery or solar backup would address power outages, and end-to-end encryption, multi-factor authentication, and tamper-resistant materials would harden security. Multi-language support and AI-driven delivery analytics are also on the roadmap.

Smart Drop shows that a low-cost IoT box with OTP access can make package delivery safer and more transparent. Conclusion, Chapter 5
